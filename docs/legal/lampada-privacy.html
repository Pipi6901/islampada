Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Lampada app ("Lampada", "we", "us") handles, and what it does not. Lampada shows a daily Bible verse on your Lock Screen, Home Screen, StandBy and Control Centre, and — if you switch it on — before you open a social app.

Short version. There is no account and no sign-up. The app works fully offline: the verses ship inside it. Your themes, colours, fonts and other choices never leave your device. Three things reach a server: your subscription purchase, handled by Apple and RevenueCat; anonymous usage statistics, handled by Google Analytics for Firebase and Amplitude — for example, how far people get through the first-run setup, what they answer there and how many Lampada widgets they use; and a few events that tell Meta whether our ads on Instagram and Facebook led to an install, a trial or a subscription. Meta gets your device's advertising identifier only if you allow tracking when your iPhone asks, and never gets your setup answers. Nothing is linked to your name, email or Apple ID, and you can switch statistics off in the app. There are no ads inside Lampada.

1. Who is responsible

The developer of Lampada is the data controller for anything described here. For any privacy question or request, write to help@pmoffice.space.

2. What stays on your device

Everything you configure in the app is stored locally, in the app's own storage on your iPhone, and is shared only with Lampada's own widget so it can draw the same verse:

  • the states you pick (anxiety, gratitude, sleeplessness and so on);
  • the language and Bible edition you read in;
  • colour sets, pictures, typefaces, sizes and alignment;
  • which places you have set up, and whether the pause before the feed is on;
  • whether you want the verse of the day as a notification, and at what time. These notifications are scheduled on your iPhone by iOS itself; nothing about them is sent to us or to anyone else;
  • which verse the app showed you and when.

Apart from your answers in the first-run setup, which are counted in the statistics described in section 3, none of this is sent anywhere. Delete the app and it is gone with it.

3. What leaves your device

Your purchase, anonymous statistics about how the app is used, and the events that measure our advertising.

Lampada offers an optional subscription, Lampada Pro. Payment is handled by Apple; we never see your card, name or email. To know whether your subscription is active, the app uses RevenueCat, a subscription infrastructure provider. To understand where people get stuck and which features they actually use, the app sends usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase, provided by Google, and to Amplitude, provided by Amplitude, Inc. We advertise Lampada on Instagram and Facebook; to learn which ads work, the app reports a few events to Meta Platforms through Meta's SDK.

The statistics never include the text of the verses you see, your Bible edition or your appearance settings. Some setup answers — choosing anxiety or sleeplessness, for example — can say something about how you feel. We look at them only in aggregate reports, never use them to single you out or for advertising, never send them to Meta, and they stop together with all other statistics when you switch statistics off. Google and Amplitude receive no name, email, phone number, Apple ID or advertising identifier. We use the version of the Firebase library built without advertising-identifier support, and we have switched off advertising storage, ad personalisation and the use of this data for advertising in Google's consent settings. Google processes this data on our behalf. It may be processed outside your country, including in the United States, under Google's data-protection terms. Event data is kept for no longer than 14 months and then deleted. Google's privacy policy is at policies.google.com/privacy, and how Firebase handles data is described at firebase.google.com/support/privacy. Amplitude processes data on our behalf in the United States; its privacy policy is at amplitude.com/privacy. Meta's privacy policy is at facebook.com/privacy/policy.

You can switch the statistics off at any time in the app: Look → App → Help improve Lampada. Once it is off, the app sends no usage statistics and no advertising events at all, including the data the analytics libraries would otherwise collect on their own.

Tracking permission. During first-run setup your iPhone asks once whether Lampada may track you. Your answer decides whether Meta receives the advertising identifier and whether RevenueCat reports renewals to Meta; the app works the same either way. You can change it at any time in iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, we rely on our legitimate interest in understanding, in aggregate, how the app is used and fixing what does not work (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), and on your consent — the tracking permission — for sharing identifiers with Meta (Article 6(1)(a)). You can object or withdraw consent at any time with the switches above.

We do not set a custom user ID and do not pass RevenueCat any attributes about you. If you allowed tracking, the app gives RevenueCat Meta's anonymous identifier and the device identifiers described above, so that RevenueCat can report subscription events to Meta; if you did not, it gives RevenueCat nothing of the kind. RevenueCat's own privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

4. What we do not do

  • No account, no registration, no email or phone number is ever requested.
  • No ads inside the app and no ad networks other than Meta, which we use only to measure our own ads for Lampada. The advertising identifier is read only if you allow tracking.
  • No analytics beyond what is described above — no session recording, no screenshots of your screen.
  • No location, no contacts, no photos, no microphone, no health data.
  • We do not sell or rent your data. The only data shared for advertising is the set of events sent to Meta described in section 3.

5. Scripture text

The verses and the short context lines are bundled with the app and read offline. The Bible editions used — the World English Bible, Luther 1912, Louis Segond 1910, Reina-Valera and the Russian Synodal translation — are in the public domain.

6. The pause before the feed

This feature is optional and off until you set it up yourself in Apple's Shortcuts app. When it runs, Shortcuts tells Lampada which app you were opening so that the "Continue" button can take you back there. That happens entirely on your device. Lampada does not record which apps you open, how often, or for how long, and nothing about it is sent anywhere.

7. Children

Lampada is rated 4+ and is safe for all ages. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. The only data that leaves the device is the purchase record, the anonymous usage statistics and the advertising events described in section 3.

8. Your rights

We hold no account and no profile about you. Usage statistics are tied only to random installation identifiers, so we cannot look them up by your name or email. Deleting the app removes your local settings and resets that identifier; statistics collected under the old one are deleted at the end of the retention period. Purchase records held by Apple and RevenueCat are governed by their own policies. You have the right to object to the processing of usage statistics: switch them off in the app as described in section 3, or write to help@pmoffice.space and we will help with any request we can act on.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
