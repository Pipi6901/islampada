Lampada — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Lampada app (“Lampada”, “we”, “us”) handles, and what it does not. Lampada shows a daily Bible verse on your Lock Screen, Home Screen, StandBy and Control Centre, and — if you switch it on — before you open a social app.

Short version. There is no account and no sign-up. The app works fully offline: the verses ship inside it. Your themes, colours, fonts and other choices never leave your device. Three things reach a server: your subscription purchase, handled by Apple and RevenueCat; anonymous usage statistics, handled by Google Analytics for Firebase — for example, how far people get through the first-run setup, what they answer there and how many Lampada widgets they use; and, if you allow notifications, a push token so that notifications can be delivered. News and offers are sent only if you turn on “News and updates”. Nothing is linked to your name, email or Apple ID, we do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites, and you can switch statistics off in the app. There are no ads inside Lampada.

1. Who is responsible

The developer of Lampada is the data controller for anything described here. For any privacy question or request, write to help@pmoffice.space.

2. What stays on your device

Everything you configure in the app is stored locally, in the app’s own storage on your iPhone, and is shared only with Lampada’s own widget so it can draw the same verse:

  • the states you pick (anxiety, gratitude, sleeplessness and so on);
  • the language and Bible edition you read in;
  • colour sets, pictures, typefaces, sizes, alignment and the app icon;
  • which places you have set up, and whether the pause before the feed is on;
  • whether you want the verse of the day as a notification, and at what time. These notifications are scheduled on your iPhone by iOS itself; nothing about them is sent to us or to anyone else;
  • your answer to “How much of your day goes to the feed?” — it is used only to show you a number and is never sent anywhere;
  • which verse the app showed you and when.

Apart from your answers in the first-run setup, which are counted in the statistics described in section 3, none of this is sent anywhere. Delete the app and it is gone with it.

3. What leaves your device

Lampada offers an optional subscription, Lampada Pro. Payment is handled by Apple; we never see your card, name or email. To know whether your subscription is active, the app uses RevenueCat, a subscription infrastructure provider. To understand where people get stuck and which features they actually use, the app sends usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase, provided by Google. This is everything that leaves your device:

  • Purchase history — which subscription was bought, renewed or refunded, and its expiry. Apple, RevenueCat. To unlock paid features on your device and to restore them if you reinstall the app or change phone.
  • An anonymous identifier generated by RevenueCat for your installation. RevenueCat. To tie a purchase to an installation. It is a random value — not your Apple ID, not your device’s advertising identifier, and not linked to your name, email or phone number.
  • Usage events: which first-run setup screens you reached; when the subscription screen was shown and which part of the app opened it; whether a free trial or a purchase started, with the plan, price and currency; why a purchase failed, if the App Store refused it; when a Lampada widget was added or removed, with its type and size only; how many Lampada widgets you have, and, once a day, that a Lampada widget is still on your screen; whether you allowed notifications, and which kind of Lampada notification you opened; which pages of the “What’s new” screen you saw; whether “News and updates” is on, and where you switched it. Google (Firebase). To see, in aggregate, where the app works and where it does not, how many people come back, and to improve it.
  • Your answers in the first-run setup: what you need most right now (comfort, a daily verse, less scrolling, or a beautiful phone) and which states you picked (for example, anxiety, gratitude or sleeplessness). If you cancel a free trial and choose to tell us why, that reason. Google (Firebase). To understand, in aggregate, who finds Lampada useful and why people leave.
  • A push token for your installation. Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging). To deliver notifications we send through Firebase. News, new features and offers are sent only if you turned on “News and updates” in the app; you can turn it off at any time in Places.
  • Data the analytics library collects on its own: a random app-instance identifier created on install, device model, iOS and app version, language, country and approximate region (derived from the IP address, which Google Analytics does not store), first launch, session start and length. Google (Firebase). The same: aggregate reports such as how many people come back the next day.

The statistics never include the text of the verses you see, your Bible edition or your appearance settings. Some setup answers — choosing anxiety or sleeplessness, for example — can say something about how you feel. We look at them only in aggregate reports, never use them to single you out or for advertising, and they stop together with all other statistics when you switch statistics off. Google receives no name, email, phone number, Apple ID or advertising identifier. We use the version of the Firebase library built without advertising-identifier support, and we have switched off advertising storage, ad personalisation and the use of this data for advertising in Google’s consent settings. Google processes this data on our behalf. It may be processed outside your country, including in the United States, under Google’s data-protection terms. Event data is kept for no longer than 14 months and then deleted. Google’s privacy policy is at policies.google.com/privacy, and how Firebase handles data is described at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

You can switch the statistics off at any time in the app: Look → App → Help improve Lampada. Once it is off, the app sends no usage statistics at all, including the data the analytics library would otherwise collect on its own.

News and updates. Notifications about new features and offers are sent only if you turn on “News and updates” — on the “What’s new” screen or in Places → In the moment. It is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off there at any time. The verse of the day and other reminders you set up do not depend on it.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, we rely on our legitimate interest in understanding, in aggregate, how the app is used and fixing what does not work (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), and on your consent for news and offers (Article 6(1)(a)). You can object or withdraw consent at any time with the switches above.

We do not set a custom user ID and do not pass RevenueCat any attributes about you. RevenueCat’s own privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

4. What we do not do

  • No account, no registration, no email or phone number is ever requested.
  • No ads inside the app, no ad networks, and no tracking: we do not read your device’s advertising identifier and do not share data with data brokers or for advertising.
  • No analytics beyond what is described above — no session recording, no screenshots of your screen.
  • No precise location, no contacts, no photos, no microphone, no health data.
  • We do not sell or rent your data.

5. Scripture text

The verses and the short context lines are bundled with the app and read offline. The Bible editions used — the World English Bible, the Russian Synodal translation, Luther 1912, Louis Segond 1910, Reina-Valera 1909, Riveduta 1927 and the Korean Revised Version (개역한글) — are in the public domain. Bíblia Livre is used under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC BY 4.0) — translation by Diego Santos, Mario Sérgio and Marco Teles; we adjusted final punctuation. The texts were obtained from eBible.org.

6. The pause before the feed

This feature is optional and off until you set it up yourself in Apple’s Shortcuts app. When it runs, Shortcuts tells Lampada which app you were opening so that the “Continue” button can take you back there. That happens entirely on your device. Lampada does not record which apps you open, how often, or for how long, and nothing about it is sent anywhere.

7. Children

Lampada is rated 4+ and is safe for all ages. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. The only data that leaves the device is the purchase record, the anonymous usage statistics and the push token described in section 3.

8. Your rights

We hold no account and no profile about you. Usage statistics are tied only to random installation identifiers, so we cannot look them up by your name or email. Deleting the app removes your local settings and resets that identifier; statistics collected under the old one are deleted at the end of the retention period. Purchase records held by Apple and RevenueCat are governed by their own policies. You have the right to object to the processing of usage statistics: switch them off in the app as described in section 3, or write to help@pmoffice.space and we will help with any request we can act on.

9. Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes.
